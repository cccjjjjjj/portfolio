## Context and problem

Traditional socioeconomic surveys are expensive and slow to update. This research explored whether public satellite indicators could strengthen poverty forecasting across the Philippines.

The study combined pixel-level nighttime radiance, Enhanced Vegetation Index, Normalized Difference Vegetation Index, and Population in the Lowest Wealth Quantile data.

## My role

I worked as a model developer and researcher within a collaborative undergraduate team. My contribution included model development, temporal analysis, research, and interpretation. The study and its reported results are team outcomes.

## Approach

The team developed a recurrent-neural-network forecasting approach using time-series satellite and socioeconomic indicators for Philippine regions. Nighttime radiance represented patterns of human activity and urban development, while vegetation indices added land-cover and environmental context.

## Reported results

The paper reported average mean absolute errors of 0.7698 for pixel radiance, 0.0044 for NDVI, 0.0023 for EVI, and 0.4878 for the poverty indicator across regions. These values belong to the collaborative study and require the paper's methodology and limitations for proper interpretation.

## Publication

The paper was accepted for oral presentation at the 6th International Conference on Big Data and Artificial Intelligence in 2023. A later version was published by Springer in 2026 as a chapter in *Machine and Computing Technologies for Sustainable Development*.

## Reflection

This project introduced me to the practical difficulty of combining geospatial, temporal, and socioeconomic data. It also showed why model results must be communicated with their source, geographic unit, and limitations intact.
