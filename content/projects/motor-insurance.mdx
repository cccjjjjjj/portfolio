## Context and problem

A single portfolio loss ratio cannot explain whether poor performance comes from claim frequency, severity, exposure, policy design, customer behaviour, vehicle profile, or geography.

I analysed a Spanish motor-insurance portfolio observed from 2022 to 2024. The work tested ten familiar risk stereotypes and classified each as supported, contradicted, or mixed based on the evidence.

## My role

I designed the analytical questions, prepared the data, created derived variables, selected the visual methods, performed the statistical checks, and wrote the decision-focused interpretation.

## Analytical structure

The ten questions supported three objectives:

1. Identify segments creating profitability pressure.
2. Find low-risk, profitable customers worth retaining.
3. Separate stereotypes from evidence that could support calibrated pricing or monitoring.

Each visual method was selected for a decision purpose. KPI matrices compared segment materiality, distribution plots exposed overlap, ridgelines revealed adverse tails, and heatmaps identified interaction risk hidden by single-variable analysis.

## Challenge

Aggregate averages can make a broad group appear risky even when the problem is concentrated in a small tail. I therefore combined segment size, loss ratio, claim frequency, claim severity, and uncertainty rather than confirming a myth from one chart.

## Outcome

The result is a reproducible visual report that moves from portfolio triage into detailed myth testing. It provides a more careful basis for retention, monitoring, and pricing conversations without presenting the exercise as a production pricing model.

## Reflection

The project reinforced a principle I use across analytics work: a visually interesting chart is not enough. The method must answer a decision question, expose uncertainty, and remain interpretable without relying on animation or memory.
